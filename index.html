Concept — the official EFC app

EFC owns the fights. It doesn't yet own the audience.

Every event, every fighter, every highlight currently lives on somebody else's platform — a broadcaster's schedule, a social feed's algorithm, a PDF on a website. An owned app turns 17 years of fight archive and a continent of fans into a direct channel EFC controls, measures and sells against.

The gap

Where the audience leaks out

This isn't a criticism of the current setup — it's a description of what a broadcaster-led distribution model can't do. Four leaks, all fixable with one product:

No fan list

Followers belong to Meta, TikTok and YouTube. If those platforms change the feed tomorrow, reach drops and there's no way to reach fans directly. An app gives EFC push notifications straight to a fan's lock screen — for free, forever.

News that can't travel

Fight announcements currently land as PDF documents. They can't be shared, indexed, notified or measured. The same story inside an app becomes a push, a share card and a data point.

An archive doing nothing between events

Ten events a year means 355 days with no live product. A searchable fight library — by fighter, by division, by finish — keeps the app open in the gap and makes every new signing a reason to go back through the catalogue.

Sponsorship priced on guesswork

WSB, The Capital and Knox currently buy arena and broadcast exposure. An app adds a measurable inventory — sponsored sections, notification takeovers, in-app activations — with real numbers attached. That's a new line item, not a new discount.

Scope — phase one

What ships

Deliberately no live pay-per-view in phase one. Live streaming is where the cost, the rights complexity and the risk sit, and it's the wrong thing to prove first. Phase one builds the audience; live gets added once there's an audience worth streaming to.

The commercial case

What it opens up

Three routes, and only one of them asks fans to pay anything:

RouteMechanic
Sponsorship inventoryA presenting partner on an owned, measurable channel — impressions, watch time and audience data attached. Something WSB, The Capital and Knox can be sold at renewal that doesn't exist today.
Premium archive tierFree highlights and news for everyone; full fights and the historic archive behind a modest monthly tier for the hardcore fanbase.
Tickets and merchandiseCard announcements land on the lock screen of everyone who has ever opened the app, ticket link one tap away. Consistently a lower cost per ticket than paid social.
Delivery

16 weeks to store

  • Discovery & content auditWhat's in the archive, what format, who publishes what internally.
  • Design directionTwo visual routes on real EFC content. One gets picked, one gets binned.
  • Full screen designs signed offEvery screen, clickable, before a line of production code.
  • Backend & CMS buildFighters, events, articles, video. EFC's team trained on it.
  • App buildiOS and Android from one codebase.
  • Content load & internal betaReal events, real fighters, EFC staff testing on their own phones.
  • Store review & launchTimed to land the week of an event card, not a quiet Tuesday.

Launching against a live event matters. An announcement week gives the app its first push notification, its first spike and its first retention curve on day one.

Next

Where this goes

What you're clicking through is a working prototype, not a mockup — the structure, the navigation and the content model are real. The next step is a short session with the EFC team to pin down two things: how much of the archive comes across at launch, and which event card the launch is timed to.

Launching against a live event matters more than launching early. An announcement week gives the app its first push notification, its first spike, and a retention curve worth reading from day one.
